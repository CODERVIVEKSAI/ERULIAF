Katalin Karikó

Field: Biochemistry & mRNA Biotechnology

Country: Hungary / United States

Key Trait: Unwavering persistence, scientific passion, and resilience

Description: Nobel laureate biochemist whose decades of pioneering research on modified mRNA enabled life-saving COVID-19 vaccines.

Katalin Karikó was born in Szolnok, Hungary, in 1955. Earning her Ph.D. at the University of Szeged, she moved to the United States in 1985. For decades, her belief that messenger RNA (mRNA) could be used to instruct cells to make therapeutic proteins met with skepticism, rejections, and academic demotions. Partnering with Drew Weissman at the University of Pennsylvania, she discovered how to chemically modify mRNA to prevent inflammatory rejection. Their breakthrough laid the groundwork for BioNTech and Pfizer's rapid COVID-19 vaccine development, earning them the 2023 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

"Science is fun. What is exciting is that there is a complexity, and then it is you who can solve it."
— Katalin Karikó
