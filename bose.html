Sir Jagadish Chandra Bose

Field: Biophysics, Botany & Radio Physics

Country: India

Key Trait: Interdisciplinary genius, open-science ethics, and precision instrumentation

Description: Pioneering Bengali polymath, physicist, biologist, and biophysicist who made seminal contributions to millimeter-wave radio signals and plant physiology.

Sir Jagadish Chandra Bose was born in Bikrampur, Bengal (now Bangladesh), in 1858. Educated at St. Xavier's College, Kolkata, and Christ's College, Cambridge, he returned to India as a Professor of Physics at Presidency College. Overcoming resource constraints and racial discrimination in research funding, Bose invented millimeter-wave wireless signaling instruments in the 1890s and later transitioned to plant physiology, proving that plants respond to external stimuli via electrical signals before his death in 1937.

"The true laboratory is the mind, where behind illusions we uncover the laws of truth."
— Sir Jagadish Chandra Bose
